§2

Switching bias correction off

Revised: the error grows after step 1

“It matters most at the first step.” (lesson §6)

For a steady gradient the uncorrected step is the corrected one times f(t) = (1 − β₁ᵗ)/√(1 − β₂ᵗ). The top recovers in about ten steps and the bottom in about a thousand, so the error grows after step 1 before it shrinks. Slide β₂ to see how long it lasts.

§5

How the best learning rate moves with width

SP fit failed the hold-out · muP held

“Under the standard parameterization it varies roughly in inverse proportion to width … under muP the minimum is at the same value at every width.” (lesson §12)

A 4-layer GPT at widths 256, 512 and 1,024, swept over 12 learning rates plus a finer second pass, with 2 seeds and 600 steps. Width 2,048 was trained but held out: the trend is fitted without it, then checked against it before anything is said about 4,096.

§6

AdamW vs Muon, both tuned

Smaller than it first looked

“Almost every optimizer claim that failed to replicate was a well tuned method measured against a badly tuned one.” (lesson §15)

Muon handles the hidden matrices and AdamW handles everything else. Muon’s step is scaled to match AdamW’s, so every arm shares one LR axis and gets the same sweep. The more carefully AdamW is tuned, the smaller Muon’s lead becomes.
