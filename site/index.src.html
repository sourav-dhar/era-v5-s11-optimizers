§2

Switching bias correction off

“It matters most at the first step.” (lesson §6)

For a steady gradient the uncorrected step is the corrected one times f(t) = (1 − β₁ᵗ)/√(1 − β₂ᵗ). The numerator recovers on β₁’s ten-step clock and the denominator on β₂’s thousand-step clock, so the error grows after step 1 before it decays.

§5

How the best learning rate moves with width

“Under the standard parameterization it varies roughly in inverse proportion to width … under muP the minimum is at the same value at every width.” (lesson §12)

A 4-layer GPT swept over 12 learning rates at widths 256, 512 and 1,024, with 2 seeds and 600 steps. Width 2,048 is trained but held out: the width law is fitted without it, then checked against it before anything is claimed about 4,096.

§6

AdamW vs Muon, both tuned

“Almost every optimizer claim that failed to replicate was a well tuned method measured against a badly tuned one.” (lesson §15)

Muon handles the hidden matrices and AdamW handles everything else. Muon’s step is RMS-matched, so both optimizers share one LR axis and get the same sweep.
